1. Introduction
Every action, no matter if it is important or not, is done for some purpose. In fact, the interaction of people with the
surrounding environment can be observed via human eyes or cameras. Through the vision system, we can understand
the action; however, it is costly to use humans in many cases. Instead, people want machines to perform the same task
as humans. Therefore, researchers have made great eﬀort to imitate the human ability of understanding actions in many
scenarios. Many systems only require dynamics collected from one view; for example, hand gestures understanding
systems often ask users to perform at a ﬁxed view only while other applications might be installed with multiple
views attached. Recently, multi-view action recognition has become attractive as the multi-sensors are widely applied
in diﬀerent real-world applications. The advantages of using multiple camera views over traditionally single view
can be listed such as: easing the localization of the actor’s position, overcoming cases in which the actor’s body is

Abstract
Human action recognition is one of the most important problems in computer vision. In this paper, we propose a novel architecture
for multi-view human action recognition. The proposal exploits the temporal features and fuses the information from diﬀerent
camera views. Based on the idea of TSN (Temporal Segment Networks) which is working with segments of videos, we recommend
aggregating scores from segments by an RNN (Recurrent Neural Network) module to enhance the quality of dynamics features.
Furthermore, the proposed architecture is designed to form a multi-branch network with each branch taking responsibility for
extracting a view-speciﬁc information, and the ﬁnal feature is formed by combining results from branches lastly. Experiments on
two datasets NUMA and MicaHandGesture have proved that the proposed architecture works eﬀectively in diﬀerent scenarios. Our
model has achieved promising results with the accuracy slightly surpass other previous works.

utilities, this paper proposes a novel architecture which contains diﬀerent branches while each branch is responsible
for extracting view-speciﬁc feature from a single view. The branch is built by inheriting the idea of the famous

2. Related works
Researchers have made signiﬁcant contributions in designing eﬀective features as well as classiﬁers for action
recognition [1, 4, 5, 7, 9].
Kong et al. [6] published their work on designing a feature called deeply learnt view-invariant features. The paper
addresses that classifying human’s action from varied views is a challenging task and proposes a model that learns
both view-speciﬁc features and shared features by using the input from many camera views. A sample-aﬃnity matrix
is then used to balance the information transfer among views and limit the information transfer across samples of
videos. Likely, DongangWang et al. [11] also exploited the beneﬁts of using multiple views in training the model of
recognizing human actions. However, the architecture is designed to hold many branches and each the input from a
view is led into its own branch. Each branch after the training process could learn the most speciﬁc information from
a view. Afterwards, a module called message passing module is set up that allows branches to interact with each other
to reﬁne the features from each branch. Finally, a system of classiﬁers is placed at each branch end to ﬁnd the ratio of
contribution of features in each branch to the ﬁnal features.
Temporal segment networks (TSN) by L. Wang et al., 2016 [13] produces an assumption that motion does not vary
suddenly in consecutive frames. Hence, by implementing sparse sampling on a long-range video the accuracy will be
increased. Furthermore, TSN works with segments of videos then combines the results from segments together for
ﬁnal prediction. In the training phases, the authors even apply some augmentation techniques to prevent the model
from lacking training data and overﬁtting that they call corner cropping and scale-jittering. However, the segments
consensus does not take into account the chronological order of the segments. Besides, Lu & Nguyen [10] has pro-
posed some improvements on the original TSN. Their work focuses on how eﬀectively the TSN model works if the
combining function at global level is replaced. The authors have else proposed a multi-level integration schema for a
two-streams model to integrate information between streams. The experimental results conﬁrmed the advantages of
LSTM module and multi-level integration schema for human action recognition.
3. Proposed methods
3.1. Basic TSN
Recent researches on human action recognition often face the same problem of modeling long-range temporal
structure due to the restriction of retrieving action features. One of the reasons is that they are only able to perform
on a single frame or a stack of frames but in short snippets. However, TSN [13] diﬀers from those methods by its
spare sampling policy to model the dynamics over the entire video. Instead of working with frames, TSN process a
series of snippets that are extracted from a full-length video. A snippet itself produces a preliminary prediction of
action classes.In the next step, a consensus is applied to aggregate the calculation on segments together to produce the
video-level prediction. The core idea of TSN is illustrated as in Figure 1. In which, a video is divided into K segments

3.2. Multi-branch TSN
The action features from diﬀerent views of the same action could share a lot of information in common; however,
they also contain their own characteristics that are only available in the corresponding views. Thus, fusing that infor-
mation might strengthen the eﬀectiveness of the features extracted. The mentioned assumption leads to the idea that
we decided to expand the TSN [13] to a multi-branch network as in Figure 2 that accepts the input from multiple cam-
era views. In the Multi-branch TSN, the Inception-BN is kept extracting low-level features of action for all branches.
Low-level features can be considered as information representing basic primitives, they can be similar between views,
so we use the shared Inception-BN for all views. From there on, the low-level feature is delivered to its branch, and
the number of branches in the network is up to the number of available views. As mentioned earlier, each branch’s
main function is to extract a view-speciﬁc feature of action; hence, it only accepts the input comes from a single
view. For the ﬁrst version, our branch inherits from the TSN by applying the policy of sparse sampling and working
with sequences of snippets instead of frames, the snippets are then aggregated by an average pooling as a consensus.
On another hand, because the Inception-BN is shared among branches, a block of 3x3 convolution is placed in each
branch as shown in Figure 2 for reﬁning most speciﬁc details from each view. Finally, the features outputted from
branches are combined by a concatenation before being fed into the classiﬁer.
3.3. Multi-branch TSN with GRU
As we discussed earlier in section 3.1, TSN [13] practices an average pooling layer to connect features from seg-
ments together. However, average pooling only calculates the mean values of features without considering the order
of extracted features. In addition, videos are similar to other kinds of sequential data, frames in a video are connected
together sequentially, and it must be an omission if that information of sequentiality is ignored. The temporal infor-
mation concealed in the frames also holds the key to motion of performers in the video. Besides, the previous work
of Lu & Nguyen [10] also proved the role of chronological order of segments in action recognition model. Therefore,
we expect that by replacing the consensus introduced in the TSN by another could enhance the accuracy of predic-
tion. Nonetheless, CNNs are not designed to process sequential data. Fortunately, RNNs with the ability to handle
sequential data and could be nicely cooperated with CNNs to reﬁne our features. On another hand, the features being
fetched from the RNNs must satisfy the following requirements: able to describe the spatial features of actions, able
to perform the temporal characteristics of motion. Therefore, we adapt a GRU (Gated recurrent unit) as our consensus

4. Experimental results
In order to evaluate our proposals, we have done experiments and evaluation on two datasets: NUMA and Mica-
HandGesture. NUMA1 (also sometimes known as NUCLA dataset) is a public and popular multi-view human action
dataset captured in Northwestern-UCLA [12]. The dataset includes 10 action categories: pick up with one hand, pick
up with two hands, drop trash, walk around, sit down, stand up, donning, doﬃng, throw, carry. Besides, this dataset
contains 1745 RGB videos s and the correlated depth frames and skeleton information performed by 10 actors, and
captured by 3 cameras. Those cameras are set up at 3 views as illustrated in Figure 5 . MicaHandGesture dataset

In our experiments, we use only RGB frames as input modality. All experiments are supported by the GeForce GTX
1080 GPU, with the conﬁguration kept unchanged as in TSN [13]. In detail, the number of epochs is set to 80 while

scenarios of evaluation are taken, which are cross-subject and cross-view, respectively. Cross-subject evaluation helps
to evaluate the model when the actor is unknown. In this experiment, the videos of the testing actor are eliminated in
the training phase. Variously, cross-view evaluation aims to test how the model performs if the testing view is unseen.
In cross-view scenarios, the videos from one view are used as testing view; those videos are eliminated in the training
phases unlike the rest of the dataset.
4.1. Cross-subject Evaluation
The NUMA action recognition dataset is performed by 10 diﬀerent subjects (actors). In this experiment, cross-
evaluation is applied, at each fold an actor is chosen to be the testing actor while the other ones are considered as
training actors, only actions which are recorded by three camera views are used in the experiment; likely, the same
strategy is applied for MicaHandGesture dataset. Multi-view is applied at both the training phase and testing phase.

of 2 videos of 2 diﬀerent training views of the same action is fed into the network for training while at testing phase
the video of the other view is doubled to feed into 2 diﬀerent branches of the network. Therefore, two branches in this

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we have proposed a novel architecture based on the TSN model [13]. In detail, our improvement
includes replacing the classical average pooling consensus by a GRU to aggregate segments of videos and expanding
basic TSN to a multi-branch network to accept multi-view input. Our experimental results on NUMA and Mica-
HandGesture datasets in two scenarios cross-view and cross-subject have proved the eﬀectiveness of our proposals.
Our proposed model provided comparative results even slightly better than other methods in both scenarios.
